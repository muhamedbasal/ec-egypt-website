Whole or split lentils: build the right bulk specification

Compare the product form, test a sample in your own process, then request a quote for the same specification.

Start with the finished product

EC’s catalogue lists whole lentils and yellow split lentils. Choose the form by the result your recipe or production line needs, rather than by colour or price alone. Compare actual samples before agreeing a bulk specification.

Lentils are traded in whole and split forms, with or without the seed coat. The USA Pulses ingredient guide (PDF) describes these forms and food applications. The two catalogue entries below identify EC’s listed products; origin, grade and current availability need confirmation for the specific inquiry.

Two starting points for a sample trial

Whole lentils — عدس بجبة

Start here when you want to assess the appearance and texture of individual lentils in the finished dish or product. Test how many remain intact after your normal cooking and handling steps.

  • Agree the required size range and colour uniformity.
  • Record split or damaged grains separately from sound whole grains.
  • Compare texture and intact-grain results after the same process.

Yellow split lentils — عدس أصفر مجروش

Start here for a trial where a broken-down or blended result, such as a soup base, is the goal. Check the sample in your recipe; a product name alone cannot guarantee the result.

  • Confirm that the quote is for split lentils, not whole lentils.
  • Assess consistency of the split pieces and fine fragments.
  • Compare final texture, colour and yield under the same process.

Turn a sample into a usable specification

The Canadian Grain Commission’s lentil grading guide separates factors such as colour, damage and foreign material. Use clear factor names when discussing a lot. Its Canadian grading system is a reference, not a statement that EC’s stock has a Canadian origin, grade or certification.

  1. Identify the sample. Record the supplier’s sample or lot reference, date received, product form and declared origin. Ask whether it represents the lot being quoted.
  2. Describe what you inspect. Note size consistency, colour, broken or damaged material, visible foreign material and packaging condition. Agree measurable acceptance criteria with the supplier; do not infer purity or safety from a photograph.
  3. Run a consistent application trial. Use the same dry input weight, preparation, equipment and endpoint for each sample. Record water used, process time, final texture and drained or finished weight. These are your trial results, not guaranteed supplier specifications.
  4. Confirm before ordering. Attach the accepted sample reference and agreed specification to the order. Ask how a lot change will be identified and agreed. Where required, request relevant lot documentation and qualified testing; a visual or kitchen trial is not a food-safety clearance.
